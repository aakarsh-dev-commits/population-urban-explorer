THE CHALLENGE

Fragmented Data in a Rapidly Urbanizing World

Population dynamics serve as one of the strongest indicators for economic health and policy planning. Yet geospatial data remains deeply fragmented — scattered across CSV tables, raster GeoTIFFs, and proprietary databases with no unified pipeline.

WHY IT MATTERS

Developing nations rely on outdated census data, making it nearly impossible to track rapid urbanization or identify vulnerable infrastructure coldspots before they become crises.

02

THE SOLUTION

A Unified Spatial Intelligence Pipeline

This project builds an open-source pipeline that merges World Bank population statistics with Natural Earth shapefiles and feeds them into three distinct analytical engines — all visualized through interactive 3D globes and 2D maps you can explore on the right.

Switch between the visualization tabs above to explore each analytical layer independently.

03

PHASE I: FOUNDATION

Data Engineering & Visualization

GeoPandas World Bank Natural Earth Globe.gl
  • Data Ingestion: Integration of Natural Earth shapefiles with World Bank population statistics (2022), normalizing country names across conflicting ISO standards.
  • Processing: Reprojection to equal-area CRS (EPSG:6933) for accurate land area computation. Population density is log-scaled to handle the 10,000× range between Monaco and Mongolia.
  • Visualization: Laser-beam 3D globe using Globe.gl with a real NASA night-time Earth texture and a live starfield background — visible in the DENSITY tab.
04

PHASE II: HOTSPOTS

PySAL Local Moran's I — Spatial Autocorrelation

PySAL / ESDA libpysal KNN Weights p < 0.05

Using PySAL's Local Moran's I statistic, every country is tested for statistically significant spatial clustering of its population density against its K=5 nearest geographic neighbors.

■ High-High (Hotspots) — dense regions surrounded by other dense neighbours.
■ Low-Low (Coldspots) — sparse regions in sparse surroundings.
■ Not Significant — no statistically meaningful pattern detected.

→ Explore this in the HOTSPOTS tab, rendered as a Globe.gl globe with the same space aesthetic.

05

PHASE III: CLUSTERS

Unsupervised Learning — KMeans Segmentation

scikit-learn KMeans (K=5) StandardScaler

KMeans clusters all countries into 5 distinct groups based on three multi-dimensional features: log population density, total area (km²), and absolute population count — all StandardScaler-normalized to treat each feature equally.

Unlike Hotspot Detection which uses geographic proximity, Clustering groups countries by their statistical similarity regardless of where they sit on the map — revealing surprising groupings across continents.

→ Explore this in the CLUSTERS tab.

06

PHASE IV: INTERPOLATION

Inverse Distance Weighting (IDW)

scipy cKDTree IDW p=2 K=8 Neighbours

Using a KD-Tree for fast nearest-neighbour lookups, IDW computes a continuous population density surface across the globe — even over oceans and uncovered regions — by weighting each training point inversely to the square of its distance.

Unlike choropleth maps that show hard country borders, IDW reveals the gradient of human density as a smooth, continuous field — making transition zones and density gradients visible at a glance.

→ Explore this in the INTERPOLATION tab as a fully interactive density heatmap.

07

PHASE V: PACKAGING

Modular & Reproducible Python Toolkit

pyproject.toml pytest ruff
  • src/ layout: Clean separation of io, preprocessing, metrics, analysis, and visualization modules.
  • Single-command pipeline: Running python main.py regenerates all 8 visualization assets end-to-end in under 90 seconds.
  • Linting: Enforced via Ruff for clean, consistent, production-grade Python.
